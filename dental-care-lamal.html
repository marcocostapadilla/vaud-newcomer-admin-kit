Dental care in Vaud: what LAMal covers, what it does not, and how to find a dentist

Why Swiss basic health insurance usually does not pay the dentist, what that means for your budget, and how to find a dentist near EPFL or Lausanne. Orientation for international students, not insurance or medical advice.

Key facts

  • Basic health insurance (LAMal / KVG) generally does not cover dental treatment. Check-ups, cleaning, fillings for cavities and braces are normally paid by you.
  • Narrow exceptions: serious, non-preventable disease of the chewing system; dental care needed because of a serious general illness, or to support its treatment; certain listed congenital disorders; and dental care after an accident if no other insurance pays.
  • Accident: if you work at least 8 hours a week for the same employer, that employer's accident insurance (LAA) is in charge (an accident at work is always the employer's); otherwise the accident cover in your LAMal policy. See Accident insurance for students.
  • Optional dental insurance is private supplementary insurance. Insurers can refuse you or add conditions based on your health.
  • Find a dentist: the Swiss Dental Association (SSO) dentist finder, or the CHUV dental service in Lausanne. Dental emergency at any hour: 0848 133 133 (Vaud on-call line).

What basic insurance (LAMal) covers for teeth

The Federal Office of Public Health (FOPH) puts it this way:

"Generally, the costs of dental treatment are not covered."

Costs are reimbursed only when dental treatment is:

Covered case (FOPH, OPAS) Example (FOPH guide or ordinance)
Needed because of a serious, non-preventable disease of the masticatory (chewing) system
Needed because of a serious general (non-dental) illness or its after-effects, or required to support and ensure the success of its medical treatmentLeukaemia, heart-valve replacement
Caused by one of the congenital disorders listed in the ordinance (art. 19a), from age 20, or before 20 if federal invalidity insurance does not cover youCleft lip and palate
Needed after an accident, when no other insurance covers the cost

FOPH adds that basic insurance does not pay for, for example, fillings for decayed teeth or orthodontic treatment (braces). The list of covered conditions is exhaustive: it is set out in articles 17 to 19a of the Health Insurance Benefits Ordinance (OPAS / KLV), and the Federal Supreme Court has confirmed that nothing outside that list is covered.

Ask before treatment: for dental care caused by the serious general illnesses listed in OPAS art. 18, the insurer pays only if it has given a special guarantee in advance, taking into account the opinion of its medical adviser. For the other exceptions, asking your health insurer before treatment whether it will pay is still the safe route.

Sources: FOPH, Health insurance: benefits and tariffs (Dental treatment) · FOPH, The compulsory health insurance system (guide, PDF) · OFSP, Soins dentaires · OPAS, art. 17 to 19a (SR 832.112.31, FR)

If you are not on Swiss LAMal (EHIC or foreign insurance)

  • EU/EFTA students using the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC): the European Commission's Switzerland page says your EHIC does not cover dental treatment, except after an accident, for a serious disease of the chewing system, or another serious illness according to Swiss law. In other words, the same narrow rules as LAMal.
  • Students exempted with a private foreign policy: what your policy pays for dental care depends on your contract. Check the policy wording or ask your insurer (To verify per policy).

See Health insurance (LAMal) for the exemption rules.

Source: European Commission, Switzerland: European Health Insurance Card

Teeth damaged in an accident

  • Which insurance pays depends on whether you work, and how much: employer's LAA if you work at least 8 hours a week for the same employer, otherwise the accident cover in your LAMal policy. FOPH says LAMal pays for accident-related dental care only when no other insurance covers it. Details: Accident insurance for students.
  • Report the accident to the insurer in charge, and keep the dentist's invoices and any report.
  • Act fast: the Vaud dentists' society (SSO-Vaud) says even badly damaged teeth can often be saved if treated correctly, so go to a dental practice or dental clinic immediately after any injury to the teeth. Outside opening hours, call 0848 133 133.

Sources: FOPH, The compulsory health insurance system (guide, PDF) · SSO-Vaud, Urgences

Optional dental insurance (supplementary)

  • What it is: supplementary insurance is optional private cover offered by health insurers on top of LAMal. FOPH lists routine dental treatment among the extra benefits it can cover.
  • How it differs from LAMal: premiums are risk-based, and the insurer may refuse to insure you or attach conditions because of your state of health. The notice period is generally different from basic insurance.
  • Before you sign, ask the insurer (in writing): what is covered (check-ups, hygienist, fillings, orthodontics), the yearly cap, the share you pay, any waiting period, and whether existing problems are excluded. Conditions vary by insurer: To verify for each offer.
  • Ask for a written offer and compare: FOPH notes that supplementary benefits can differ significantly from one insurer to another.

Source: FOPH, The compulsory health insurance system (guide, PDF)

Paying yourself: how dental bills work

Most students pay the dentist directly. The SSO explains how fees are set:

  • Points x point value: every treatment has a number of tariff points. The bill is points multiplied by the practice's point value in CHF.
  • Private patients: the practice sets its point value, but for SSO-member dentists it is capped at CHF 1.70. Each practice must display its point value and print it on the bill.
  • Other cases: accident, military and invalidity insurance cases use a point value of CHF 1; cases paid by LAMal still use the older catalogue with CHF 3.10 per point.
  • SSO example: a periodic check-up (tariff position 4.0010) is 48.8 points; at a point value of CHF 1 that is CHF 48.80. Your private bill depends on your practice's point value and on what is done (X-rays, cleaning and so on are separate positions).
  • Ask for a cost estimate (estimation d'honoraires) before any larger treatment. For bigger work you can ask one or more practices for an estimate; SSO says the total cost depends much more on the type and extent of the treatment chosen than on individual prices or the point value.
  • Missed appointments: SSO says a patient who misses an appointment without cancelling at least 24 hours ahead must compensate the dentist, but only if the practice could not use that time for another patient.
  • The bill should list each treatment by date, tooth number, tariff position and points, plus materials and lab work.

Bills usually come as QR-bills. See Paying bills: QR-bill and eBill.

Source: SSO, Tarifs

Aged 16 to 20? SSO-Vaud runs an "adolescents vaudois" voucher: CHF 20 for a check-up and two X-rays at an SSO-Vaud member dentist, handed out in schools and at member practices, and downloadable from the SSO-Vaud page. The voucher says the first check-up must take place between ages 16 and 18 and the second between 18 and 20. It does not print an expiry date or a schooling condition; To verify with the practice before booking if you did not go to school in Vaud. See SSO-Vaud, voucher news item.

Budget

EPFL's student budget table has no separate dental line. It lists dental expenses under Other expenses, together with personal effects, mobile phone, travel and outings: CHF 250 per month / CHF 3,000 per year for the whole category. A single larger treatment can use up that line, so ask for an estimate first.

Source: EPFL, Financing your studies (approximate budget)

Finding a dentist in Vaud

Where What the official page says
SSO, Dentist finder (Trouver un médecin-dentiste)National directory of SSO-member dentists. Search by place; the advanced search includes a languages spoken filter.
État de Vaud, Médecins et médecins-dentistesVaud has dentists in private practices, private dental clinics, school dental clinics and a dental and oral medicine centre at the CHUV. All dentists need a cantonal authorisation to practise.
CHUV, Service de médecine dentaireLausanne university hospital dental service. Same services as town practices at the same tariffs. Appointments Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 17:00; request online (preferred) or by phone +41 21 314 47 47 (phone reception 08:45 to 11:15 and 14:00 to 15:00); adult service at Rue du Bugnon 44, Lausanne. Emergencies by appointment only (email or phone). Bring ID and your insurance card; you pay on site.

Tips:

  • Many practices list the languages they speak; use the SSO filter if you need English.
  • Ask the practice for its point value and an estimate when you book.
  • For a doctor (not a dentist), see GP, pharmacy and on-call care.

Sources: SSO, Dentist finder · État de Vaud, Médecins et médecins-dentistes · CHUV, Service de médecine dentaire · CHUV, Nos contacts

Dental emergency

  • Severe pain, broken tooth or other dental accident, and your dentist cannot be reached: call the Vaud on-call line (Centrale téléphonique des médecins de garde, CTMG) on 0848 133 133. SSO-Vaud says it answers 24/7 and puts you in touch with the on-call dentist for your region.
  • Life-threatening situation (for example a serious head or face injury): use the emergency numbers on Health emergencies.

Sources: SSO-Vaud, Service de garde dentaire · État de Vaud, Médecins et médecins-dentistes

If money is tight, or something goes wrong

  • Talk to your dentist first: SSO says there are usually several treatment options at different costs for the same problem.
  • Financial help: SSO says social assistance services can pay for dental care that is medically necessary, appropriate and economical for people in financial difficulty, and that some communal funds, charities and foundations help people who do not qualify for social assistance. At EPFL, the Social consultation can help you find the right resource: consultation.sociale@epfl.ch (see also GP, pharmacy and on-call care).
  • Disagreement with a dentist: SSO-Vaud has a mediation commission for disputes between patients and SSO-Vaud member dentists (voluntary, confidential). SSO says patients and dentists must first try to settle the matter between themselves; the mediation step is partly free, while later steps (fee review, expert opinion, conciliation) are generally not free. The exact fees are not published: To verify with SSO-Vaud.

Sources: SSO, Médecine dentaire sociale · SSO, Tarifs (complaints section) · SSO-Vaud, Commission de médiation
