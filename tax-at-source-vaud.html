Tax at source in Vaud (impôt à la source)

Last checked: 5 October 2026. Unofficial student guide, not EPFL or government advice: confirm on the linked official pages before you act. Unfamiliar abbreviation? See the Glossary.

Focused basics for international students and newcomers in Vaud who take a job while holding a B, L, or similar permit without a C permit. Orientation only, not tax advice. This kit does not publish withholding rates; use Vaud’s official calculators or ask ACI / your employer.

Companion pages: Student tax · Residence tax vs income tax · AVS number & payslips · Working while studying

What “tax at source” means

Impôt à la source (withholding tax) is income tax that your employer deducts from your salary each pay period and remits to the cantonal tax administration (Administration cantonale des impôts, ACI).

Vaud calls people in this regime sourciers (withholding taxpayers).

It is not the same as:

Who is a sourcier in Vaud?

ACI’s sourciers page distinguishes two main groups.

1. Domiciled or staying in Switzerland (no C permit)

Foreign workers who are domiciled or staying in Switzerland for tax purposes without an establishment permit (C) are withheld on income from employed work in Switzerland.

ACI names, among others, permits B, L, F, N, Ci, and people working without a stay authorisation.

For most EPFL-area students on a student B (or L) who take a paid job, this is the usual track: the employer withholds on the Swiss salary.

2. Not domiciled in Switzerland

Workers whose main tax domicile is abroad (any nationality, including Swiss) can also be withheld on Swiss employment income (short stays, secondary domicile with return abroad, certain frontier cases, and other categories listed by ACI). Frontier rules with France have extra conditions; see ACI if that is your situation.

Sources: Personnes imposées à la source / Sourciers · Les impôts pour les jeunes

Students: working vs not working

Your situation What official pages support
B/L (etc.) student with a Swiss job, no C permitEmployer withholds impôt à la source on that employment income. Check the payslip and the annual salary certificate (Form 11, field for withholding).
B/L student with no jobNothing to withhold on salary (ACI ties withholding to income from employed work in Switzerland). Federal and Vaud tax law say that a person who keeps their home (domicile) abroad and is in Switzerland only to attend a school or university does not become tax-domiciled or tax-resident here (LIFD art. 3 para. 4; Vaud LI art. 3 para. 4). Both conditions count: your home stays abroad, and studying is the only reason you are here. If your situation is different (for example your family lives in Vaud, you no longer have a home abroad, or you stay on after your studies), you may be tax-domiciled in Vaud, and the ordinary filing duty can then apply (Ma situation personnelle: every person from 18, students included). If ACI sends you a tax return or a letter, do not ignore it: fill it in, or contact ACI and explain your situation. What your employer does

  1. Registers you for withholding when you are a sourcier.
  2. Applies the barème (tariff) that matches your situation (ACI publishes annual instructions and Excel calculators for employers and taxpayers).
  3. Deducts the tax from each salary and remits it to ACI.
  4. Issues salary statements / the annual salary certificate (ESTV Form 11), including the withholding total where applicable.

This kit does not quote withholding percentages or tariff tables. They change by year and personal situation. Vaud publishes calculators on the sourciers page (files labelled by tax year, including 2026 when last checked). Open the year that matches your payslip.

Hub for employers and sourciers: Impôt à la source (ACI)

When ordinary assessment still applies (TOU)

Even if you are withheld, ACI describes taxation ordinaire ultérieure (TOU) paths for people domiciled in Vaud for tax purposes.

Mandatory TOU (examples ACI lists)

  • Gross annual income subject to withholding for you (or your jointly assessed spouse in the same household) exceeds CHF 120,000.
  • You have other income not covered by withholding (examples ACI gives: certain pensions, self-employment income, real-estate income).
  • You have taxable wealth (movable and/or immovable, in Switzerland and/or abroad).

Withholding continues; amounts already withheld are credited against the ordinary assessment.

TOU on request

If none of the mandatory grounds apply, you may request TOU in writing by 31 March of the year after the tax year (ACI form on the sourciers page). Once validly filed, the request cannot be withdrawn, and TOU continues until you leave the withholding regime. ACI warns the final bill can be higher or lower than pure withholding.

Leaving withholding for ordinary assessment mid-situation

ACI states you move to ordinary assessment for the whole tax period when, for example, you receive a C permit, or you marry a Swiss national / C-permit holder (details and timing on the sourciers page). Withholding stops from the month after the triggering event; amounts already withheld are credited without interest.

Source: Personnes imposées à la source / Sourciers

Practical first steps if a payslip looks odd:

  1. Keep monthly payslips and the annual salary certificate.
  2. Ask payroll which barème and civil-status codes they used.
  3. Compare with Vaud’s calculator for that tax year (sourciers page).
  4. Contact ACI source desk if needed: info.aci-source@vd.ch (ACI publishes this address; formal réclamations must go by signed postal mail, not email).
  5. Office for Lausanne / West Lausanne districts (from ACI page): Rue Caroline 11bis, CP 1142, 1001 Lausanne, tel. 021 316 20 65 (opening hours on the page).

Payslip and Form 11 pointers

ESTV’s salary-certificate guide (valid from 1 January 2026) includes ch. 12, total withholding tax deducted for foreign employees without an establishment permit (C) (and other cases the guide names). Monthly student payslips usually show a matching impôt à la source line when you are withheld.
