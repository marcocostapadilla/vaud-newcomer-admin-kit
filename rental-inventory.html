Rental inventory tip sheet (état des lieux)

What this is

When you move into a private flat or room, you and the landlord (or agency) usually walk through the place and record its condition and the furniture. In French this is the état des lieux (entry / exit inspection) plus an inventaire of furniture when the place is furnished.

EPFL’s housing guidance (written for people who rent to students, but the steps are the same for you as tenant) says to:

  • Set a date that works for both sides.
  • Walk through and note existing defects.
  • Validate the furniture inventory.
  • Sign and date the documents in duplicate (one copy each).

At departure: check cleanliness, compare with the entry inspection, check the inventory again, sign in duplicate, then free the rental guarantee if everything is in order.

Federal Code of Obligations (pointers)

On restitution of the rented object, art. 267a CO (Fedlex) requires the landlord, when the tenant returns the premises, to check the condition and immediately notify the tenant of defects the tenant is liable for. If the landlord neglects that, the tenant is discharged for defects that a normal check would have found (hidden defects: notify when discovered).

Official text: Fedlex, Code of Obligations, art. 267a (open with a JS-capable browser; French/Italian/German consolidations on the same ELI).

To verify: exact English wording on the live Fedlex page before quoting in a dispute, this kit only points you there.

Related: return the premises in a condition resulting from contractual use (art. 267 CO). Deposit cap / blocked account: see Rental deposit (art. 257e CO + EPFL Vaud practice notes).

What this page does not do

  • It does not provide a fill-in form template (use the landlord/agency form, or ask Chambre Vaudoise Immobilière materials if you are the landlord, EPFL points owners there).
  • It does not invent cleaning fees, “normal wear” CHF amounts, or how long a deposit takes to return.
