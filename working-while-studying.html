Working while studying

What this page is for

Short orientation for EPFL / Vaud newcomers on permit B for studies and paid work. Confirm every rule on the linked SEM and EPFL pages before you accept a job — this kit is not legal advice.

Federal baseline (SEM / Art. 38 ASEO)

The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) FAQ states that if you live in Switzerland to train or study at a university or university of applied sciences, the cantonal authorities may approve supplementary work under Art. 38 ASEO (Ordinance on Admission, Period of Stay and Employment / VZAE), with conditions:

  • Foreign students may work for a maximum of 15 hours a week outside of the holidays, beginning no sooner than six months after the start of their course.
  • The university must confirm the work is compatible with studies and will not delay completion.
  • An application must be submitted by the employer.
  • Pay and working conditions must meet applicable regulations.

Source: SEM — FAQ Working (“I am studying in Switzerland. Do I have the right to work…”) (last modification 05.07.2026 on that page)

SEM points you to the cantonal labour market and immigration authorities for further information.

Note: The SEM FAQ answer above does not spell out the EU/EFTA timing exception; EPFL does (next section). Read both.

How EPFL states it (practical for students here)

EPFL’s Study management page Working aligns with the 15-hour rule and spells out the EU/EFTA vs third-country timing:

  • Foreign students: maximum 15 hours per week during courses, and full-time during university vacations.
  • EU/EFTA nationals: employment may begin from the start of their stay.
  • Non-EU/EFTA nationals: must wait 6 months before taking up employment, except student assistants employed in research connected to their field of study.

EPFL also notes the heavy study load (~50–60 hours/week including personal study) and that reconciling work and studies is difficult.

Source: EPFL — Working

Procedure EPFL describes for B-permit students (regular paid work)

  1. Ask your employer to fill in the employment form (required even if the job lasts under 3 months). Forms for EU/EFTA and third-country students are linked from the EPFL Working page.
  2. Go to the Student Services Desk with the form. A certificate is issued only if hours stay within 15 h/week during semesters and full-time during academic holidays.
  3. Submit both documents to the competent office (address on the form).

EPFL warns that failure to comply may lead to refusal to extend the residence permit.

Source: same EPFL — Working page

Scholarships

EPFL notes that certain grants may restrict employment. Check restrictions with the body that issued your grant.

Source: EPFL — Working

Tax and insurance side effects (pointers only)

  • Tax: foreign students on a B permit typically have impôt à la source (withholding) deducted from salary. EPFL states that if annual income does not exceed CHF 28,800, you may request a tax refund from the canton of residence between 1 January and 31 March. For Vaud, EPFL points to the Administration cantonale des impôts — Section de l’impôt à la source, with salary statements, permit copy, EPFL registration certificate, and bank details. See also this kit’s Student tax page.
  • LAMal: starting work can change health-insurance obligations (especially for EU/EFTA/UK students who were using EHIC while not working). See LAMal and official FOPH guidance.
  • AVS: employment income brings AVS contributions via payroll. See AVS.

Sources: EPFL — Working; kit pages above

After graduation (job search)

  • SEM FAQ: third-country nationals who graduated from a Swiss higher education institution have a six-month period from the end of their course/training to look for a job matching their qualifications (admission still subject to overriding scientific or economic interest, in derogation of precedence). See SEM FAQ Working.
  • EPFL post-graduation page: foreign nationals can obtain a six-month residency permit to look for a qualification-consistent job; during that period work is limited to maximum 15 hours per week; the six-month permit cannot be extended. Conditions include EPFL end-of-study confirmation, financial means, and suitable housing.
  • EU/EFTA graduates also have free-movement rights under the FMP agreement (EPFL summarises this separately on the same page) — do not treat the third-country job-search track as your only path.

Sources:

  • EPFL — Post-graduation stay
  • SEM — FAQ Working (job search after Swiss studies)

Checklist before you start a job

  1. Confirm your nationality path (EU/EFTA vs third country) and whether the 6-month wait applies (and whether the research-assistant exception applies).
  2. Confirm hours stay within 15 h/week in term / full-time in holidays (EPFL / SEM).
  3. Have the employer start the form / application (Art. 38 ASEO / EPFL procedure).
  4. Get the EPFL Student Desk certificate when required.
  5. File with the competent cantonal office.
  6. Check grant, tax, LAMal, and AVS knock-ons.
