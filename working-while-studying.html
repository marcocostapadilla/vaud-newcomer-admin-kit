Working while studying

What this page is for

Short orientation for EPFL / Vaud newcomers on permit B for studies and paid work (side job and mandatory internship). Confirm every rule on the linked SEM, OASA, État de Vaud, and EPFL pages before you accept a contract.

Federal vs SECO: the 15-hour student side-job rule is set in Art. 38 OASA (ASEO) and explained by SEM, not on a dedicated SECO “student hours” page. In Vaud, the Service de l’emploi (SDE) authorises the accessory activity; EPFL’s internship pages point employers there. SECO appears in SEM’s FAQ mainly for other labour-market topics (e.g. recognised au-pair agencies).

Federal baseline (SEM / Art. 38 OASA)

The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) FAQ states that if you live in Switzerland to train or study at a university or university of applied sciences, the cantonal authorities may approve supplementary work under Art. 38 OASA (Ordinance on Admission, Period of Stay and Employment / ASEO), with conditions:

  • Foreign students may work for a maximum of 15 hours a week outside of the holidays, beginning no sooner than six months after the start of their course.
  • The university must confirm the work is compatible with studies and will not delay completion.
  • An application must be submitted by the employer.
  • Pay and working conditions must meet applicable regulations (Art. 22 FNIA / LEI).

Art. 38 OASA (French Fedlex text, mirrored on droit-bilingue) matches that list: school certificate, ≤ 15 h/week outside holidays, employer request, pay/conditions.

Sources:

  • SEM — FAQ Working (“I am studying in Switzerland. Do I have the right to work…”) (last modification 05.07.2026 on that page)
  • Fedlex — OASA (RS 142.201) (open Art. 38 in the consolidated text)
  • Art. 38 OASA bilingual excerpt (convenience mirror of the same article)

SEM points you to the cantonal labour market and immigration authorities for further information.

Note: The SEM FAQ answer above does not spell out the EU/EFTA timing exception; EPFL and Vaud do (next sections). Read all three.

Canton of Vaud (SPOP / SDE)

État de Vaud — Séjour pour études (third-country track page; accessory-activity rules apply to students of university / HES / HEP schools):

  • Accessory work may be authorised no sooner than six months after the start of the programme.
  • The school must confirm the activity will not delay the planned training.
  • Cap: 15 hours per week, or full-time during school holidays.
  • The activity must be announced in advance to the Service de la population (SPOP) and authorised by the Service de l’emploi (SDE) of the competent canton.

Documents Vaud lists for taking up accessory work (from 6 months of stay), among others:

  • Form 1350 « Demande de permis de séjour avec activité lucrative » (+ annexes, in duplicate)
  • Copy of the employment contract
  • Written school confirmation that the accessory activity will not delay studies

Competent authorities (Vaud page): SPOP for entry/stay; SDE in principle for authorising the accessory activity.

Source: État de Vaud — Séjour pour études

To verify: (for your case) whether your nationality path (EU/EFTA vs third country) changes the form set or the announcement-only vs full-authorisation track — follow EPFL’s forms and Vaud/SDE instructions for your permit, not a one-size summary.

How EPFL states it (practical for students here)

EPFL’s Study management page Working aligns with the 15-hour rule and spells out the EU/EFTA vs third-country timing:

  • Foreign students: maximum 15 hours per week during courses, and full-time during university vacations.
  • EU/EFTA nationals: employment may begin from the start of their stay.
  • Non-EU/EFTA nationals: must wait 6 months before taking up employment, except student assistants employed in research connected to their field of study.

EPFL also notes the heavy study load (~50–60 hours/week including personal study) and that reconciling work and studies is difficult. It quotes Directive 4.7.1 (student / construction / temporary assistants), Art. 9, for the same limits.

Source: EPFL — Working

Procedure EPFL describes for B-permit students (regular paid work)

  1. Ask your employer to fill in the employment form (required even if the job lasts under 3 months). Forms for EU/EFTA and third-country students are linked from the EPFL Working page.
  2. Go to the Student Services Desk with the form. A certificate is issued only if hours stay within 15 h/week during semesters and full-time during academic holidays.
  3. Submit both documents to the competent office (address on the form).

EPFL warns that failure to comply may lead to refusal to extend the residence permit.

Source: same EPFL — Working page

Mandatory industry internship (Art. 39 OASA) — different track

A compulsory internship that is part of your Master programme is not the same as a 15-hour side job. EPFL Recruiting (Calendar and Conditions) states, pursuant to Art. 39 OASA:

  • Foreign EPFL students with a valid residence permit may do a full-time paid internship if it is a mandatory part of their studies, but they still need labour-market authorisation.
  • Third-country (non-EU/EFTA): the company applies to the competent labour-market authority; EPFL issues an attestation that the internship is mandatory and that you remain registered; do not start until a positive decision.
  • EU/EFTA: free-movement principle for the internship duration; the internship must be announced to the inhabitants’ control of your commune or of the canton where the internship takes place; it may start once announced.
  • Master’s projects in a company under EPFL professor supervision do not require a working authorisation (EPFL).

EPFL also notes, for information, that Swiss authorities (Service de l’emploi) expect an internship in Switzerland to be paid about CHF 2,000 / month, and that this will be a requirement to deliver working authorisation for students from outside the EU. Treat that as EPFL’s summary of SDE practice — confirm with the employer / SDE for your file; do not invent a different amount here.

Vaud contacts EPFL lists: Service de l’emploi · Service de la population

Scholarships

EPFL notes that certain grants may restrict employment. Check restrictions with the body that issued your grant.

Source: EPFL — Working

Tax and insurance side effects (pointers only)

  • Tax: foreign students on a B permit typically have impôt à la source (withholding) deducted from salary. EPFL states that if annual income does not exceed CHF 28,800, you may request a tax refund from the canton of residence between 1 January and 31 March. For Vaud, EPFL points to the Administration cantonale des impôts — Section de l’impôt à la source, with salary statements, permit copy, EPFL registration certificate, and bank details. See also this kit’s Student tax page.
  • LAMal: starting work can change health-insurance obligations (especially for EU/EFTA/UK students who were using EHIC while not working). See LAMal and official FOPH guidance.
  • AVS: employment income brings AVS contributions via payroll. See AVS.

Sources: EPFL — Working; kit pages above

After graduation (job search)

  • SEM FAQ: third-country nationals who graduated from a Swiss higher education institution have a six-month period from the end of their course/training to look for a job matching their qualifications (admission still subject to overriding scientific or economic interest, in derogation of precedence). See SEM FAQ Working.
  • EPFL post-graduation page: foreign nationals can obtain a six-month residency permit to look for a qualification-consistent job; during that period work is limited to maximum 15 hours per week; the six-month permit cannot be extended. Conditions include EPFL end-of-study confirmation, financial means, and suitable housing.
  • EU/EFTA graduates also have free-movement rights under the FMP agreement (EPFL summarises this separately on the same page) — do not treat the third-country job-search track as your only path.
  • Vaud also publishes pages on the six-month job-search stay and facilitated admission for Swiss HEI graduates — use those when you graduate.

Sources:

  • EPFL — Post-graduation stay
  • SEM — FAQ Working (job search after Swiss studies)
  • État de Vaud — Séjour 6 mois recherche d’emploi (diplômés HE suisse)

Checklist before you start a job or internship

  1. Decide which track you are on: 15 h side job (Art. 38) vs mandatory full-time internship (Art. 39) vs Master project under EPFL professor (EPFL: no work authorisation).
  2. Confirm your nationality path (EU/EFTA vs third country) and whether the 6-month wait applies (and whether the research-assistant exception applies).
  3. For side jobs: confirm hours stay within 15 h/week in term / full-time in holidays (EPFL / SEM / Vaud).
  4. Have the employer start the form / application (Art. 38 or 39 / EPFL procedure).
  5. Get the EPFL Student Desk certificate or internship attestation when required.
  6. File / announce with SPOP and get SDE authorisation when Vaud requires it — do not start third-country work or internship until authorised.
  7. Check grant, tax, LAMal, and AVS knock-ons.
