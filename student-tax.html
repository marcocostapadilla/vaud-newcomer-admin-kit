Student tax in Vaud: withholding vs ordinary assessment

Last checked: 5 Oct 2026. Unofficial student guide, not EPFL or government advice: confirm on the linked official pages before you act. Unfamiliar abbreviation? See the Glossary.

Two different systems

In Vaud, how you pay tax depends mainly on your permit / nationality, not on whether you are a student.

Ordinary assessment (déclaration d’impôt / “classic” taxation) You file an annual tax return. This applies if you are:

  • a Swiss national, or
  • holder of a C permit (settlement permit),

even if you are a student, apprentice, employee, unemployed, or have no income. Filing a return does not automatically mean you will owe tax.

Source: Les impôts pour les jeunes | État de Vaud

Withholding tax (impôt à la source) Your employer deducts tax from each salary payment and remits it to the cantonal tax administration. You often do not receive a blank annual return.

This generally applies to foreign workers who live or stay in Switzerland without a C permit , including people with permits such as B, L, F, N, Ci.

Source: same Vaud page; also Ma situation personnelle | État de Vaud

Cross-border / non-resident workers can also be subject to withholding on Swiss employment income (separate rules). See Vaud’s withholding pages for details.

Not working on a B or L permit?

Vaud’s “jeunes” page explains filing for Swiss nationals and C permits, and withholding for foreign workers without a C permit. It does not spell out a separate rule for a non-working B/L student.

What the official pages do say (checked 5 Oct 2026): withholding applies to foreign workers without a C permit on the income from their employed work in Switzerland, so with no job nothing is withheld (Personnes imposées à la source). Vaud's Ma situation personnelle page, under Apprentice / Student, states that from age 18 every person, including students and people with no activity or no income, must file a return each year, with no permit exception named.

Practical guidance: if you are on a B or L study permit and not working, expect the ordinary filing duty to apply and do not assume you are exempt. If no return form reaches you in January, confirm with the Administration cantonale des impôts (ACI) what is expected of you. If you take a student job, tax is usually withheld from your salary (impôt à la source).

ACI contact details are on the Vaud tax pages above (call centre: +41 21 316 00 00; have your taxpayer number ready when you have one).

Practical notes for students under ordinary assessment

  • From the year after you turn 18, Vaud expects an annual return (via VaudTax online). Legal filing deadline cited by Vaud for the return: 15 March, with a tolerance until 30 June; an extra extension can be requested via e-Délai (see the official deadline pages for current rules).
  • Keep documents such as salary certificates, bank tax statements, and anything labelled “fiscal”.
  • Scholarships must be declared; Vaud states they are not taxable if they are not tied to a counterpart performance and the recipient is in need.
  • Low or zero income can still mean CHF 0 tax after deductions , but you may still need to file if you are under ordinary assessment.

Source: Les impôts pour les jeunes

Advanced: when withholding taxpayers may still need an ordinary assessment

If you are taxed at source and domiciled in Vaud for tax purposes, an ordinary subsequent assessment (taxation ordinaire ultérieure / TOU) can be mandatory in cases set out by the canton (examples discussed officially include high gross withholding income , Vaud cites a CHF 120,000 threshold for the taxpayer or jointly assessed spouse , or taxable wealth / income not covered by withholding). In other cases you may request an ordinary subsequent assessment in writing by 31 March of the year after the tax year.

Withholding continues; amounts already withheld are credited against the tax calculated on the return.

Sources:

  • Ma situation personnelle
  • Personnes imposées à la source / Sourciers | État de Vaud

Tip for newcomers: check your permit letter and your first payslip (look for “impôt à la source” / withholding). If unsure, contact ACI with your taxpayer number when you have one.
