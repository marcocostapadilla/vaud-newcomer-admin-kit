Opening a bank account, practical checklist

Why open one early

Many Swiss day-to-day steps (rent deposits, salary, scholarships, TWINT) are easier with a Swiss account. Student / youth packages exist; terms, ages, and documents change, confirm on each bank’s own page.

Documents to bring (checklist)

Use this list before you go to a branch or start an online application:

EPFL notes that the Residents’ registration office attestation (while you wait for the official permit) allows you to open a bank account. The attestation is not a travel document, it will not let you re-enter Switzerland if you leave. See Permit & commune.

UBS (international students, official FAQ on their youth/students page): passport/ID; proof of residence in Switzerland (permit B, C, Ci or L, or municipal registration confirmation); and enrolment proof (student ID card or current enrolment confirmation). Open via the UBS app or at a branch.

Source: EPFL, Residence permit; UBS, Youth and students account (also consistent with the kit Bank section)

Suggested order of operations

  1. Register at your commune’s Contrôle des habitants (EPFL/Lausanne: within 14 days; Renens/canton: within 8 days, follow your commune). Ask for the attestation.
  2. Print a recent enrolment certificate from IS-Academia.
  3. Open the account with passport + enrolment proof + permit or attestation.
  4. Once the account exists, update salary / scholarship / TWINT / rent-deposit details as needed.

Cross-links: Permit & commune · Bank · Phone (Swiss number often useful for banking apps)

Paying bills once the account is open (QR-bill, eBill, TWINT): see Paying Swiss bills.

What this checklist does not cover

  • Comparing interest rates or recommending a bank
  • Paid premium packages
  • Exact fee tables (always read the bank’s price list)
  • Non-student business accounts

To verify: whether your specific commune attestation wording is accepted by every bank branch, ask the bank if unsure (EPFL and UBS both mention municipal registration confirmation / attestation as acceptable proof of residence in principle).
