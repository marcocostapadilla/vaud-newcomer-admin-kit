How newcomers at EPFL can take French courses on campus: who can enrol, which modules exist, the next registration windows, and what to do if you missed one. Orientation only; the Language Centre's own pages are authoritative.

Companion pages: Start here · IS-Academia basics · CAMIPRO

What it is

The EPFL Language Centre teaches French, English, German and Italian to the EPFL community, in semester modules and short intensive courses. It is located on campus (CE 2 445) and is managed by UNIL.

For EPFL students, modules are free (financed by EPFL). You only pay for course books and any external exam fee.

Source: EPFL Language Centre

Just arrived and missed autumn? If you did pre-register, you may still join a group in the first two or three weeks of the semester when level, timetable and space allow (otherwise a waiting list). If you did not register, plan for the winter intensive or spring window, and use the free self-study options below in the meantime.

How to register

  1. During the registration window, open Register online on the Language Centre site (an IS-Academia pre-registration form) and choose French.
  2. Log in with your EPFL (Gaspar) username and password.
  3. Choose one module for the language.
  4. Complete every step: your previous learning and goals, a self-assessment, the placement test (several pages; do them all), and your weekly availability (semester modules). The placement test is skipped only if you are a complete beginner or took it less than 6 months ago. Skipping it otherwise means you will not be placed.
  5. After the deadline you get an email saying whether you are placed. Check details (time, teacher, room) in your online file; instructions for the first class arrive two or three days before the start.

Limits: you may register for up to 3 languages but are placed in at most 2 groups, and only one module per language. Pre-registration does not guarantee a place.

If you are not placed, go to the Language Centre reception: they may find another group, add you to a waiting list (closed after 2-3 weeks), or suggest guided self-study.

Sources: Dates, registration and courses · FAQ

Attendance and attestations

  • By registering you commit to attend regularly for the whole semester; tell your teacher in advance if you must miss a class.
  • For participants who attend free of charge, two consecutive unexcused absences mean automatic exclusion from the group.
  • An attestation requires 80% presence and validation of the Practice Activities sessions. There are two types: participation only, or with final grade and ECTS credits.

Whether those ECTS count toward your own study plan is decided by your section.

Free self-study (no registration)

  • Multimedia Centre (EMA): free-access periods with self-study material sorted by level.
  • PlurieL: the Language Centre's online learning platform, usable any time.
  • Tandem: meet a partner with another mother tongue to swap languages, via the UNIL Tandem programme. Link: UNIL Tandem.
  • Ça joue!: free online French basics for incoming mobility students, built by the Language Centre: Ça joue!.

Source: All modules in French

Special case: intensive French for new Bachelor students

EPFL also runs a two-week on-campus intensive French course before the academic year (2026: 17 to 28 Aug, registration 26 May to 12 Jul 2026). It is open only to holders of a Swiss-German or Swiss-Italian high-school certificate, so most international newcomers use the Language Centre routes above instead.

Source: EPFL, New Bachelor students

Not eligible? (partners, family, non-EPFL)

The Language Centre programme is reserved for the EPFL community. Its FAQ points others to:

  • Université populaire and the Association vaudoise des écoles privées (AVDEP) for schools in the Lausanne area;
  • UNIL's intensive French courses (Le FIL).

The Canton of Vaud's newcomer site lists French courses run by associations and subsidised by the State, aimed at small budgets (some with childcare), and points to private schools via AVDEP: Vaud Bienvenue, Cours de français (FR). Fees and schedules are on each course listing.

The same Vaud page notes that some permit steps (for example a B permit request or renewal, an early C permit, or naturalisation) can require a certificate of French level; check what your own procedure asks for.
