Bike, e-bike and e-scooter road rules

Last checked: 10 October 2026. Unofficial student guide, not EPFL or government advice: confirm on the linked official pages before you act. Unfamiliar abbreviation? See the Glossary.

The Swiss rules for riding a bike, a slow or fast e-bike, or an e-scooter: age, licence, helmet, number plate, lights, where to ride, and alcohol.

Key facts

  • No e-vehicle under 14. Slow e-bikes (25 km/h) and e-scooters (20 km/h): category M licence from 14 to 16, no licence from 16. Fast e-bikes (45 km/h): always a licence (at least category M).
  • Helmet: compulsory only on fast e-bikes; strongly recommended on everything else.
  • Lights on, day and night, on all e-bikes and e-scooters.
  • Number plate: only fast e-bikes (yellow plate, insurance). Ordinary bikes, slow e-bikes and e-scooters have none, and the old bike vignette no longer exists.
  • Pavements are off-limits for all e-vehicles and for adult cyclists, unless signs allow it. Children up to 12 may cycle on the pavement where there is no cycle lane or path.

Fast e-bikes also need a speedometer. Many e-scooters sold online do not meet Swiss approval rules and cannot be used on public roads (ASTRA). In Vaud, a fast e-bike plate is requested from the cantonal vehicle office (link below).

Ordinary bikes: no age limit or licence. They need two brakes, white and red reflectors and reflectors on the pedals. In the dark, rain, fog and tunnels you need a steady white front light and red rear light. A helmet is recommended, not compulsory.

2. Where to ride

  • Cycle paths and lanes are compulsory where they exist, for bikes, slow e-bikes and e-scooters.
  • Fast e-bikes must also use them, unless a sign says mopeds may choose (optional) or are banned.
  • No pavements for e-vehicles, e-scooters included, unless signs allow it. E-scooter riders follow the same traffic rules as cyclists.
  • Children up to 12 may ride a bike on the pavement when there is no cycle lane or path, giving way to pedestrians.
  • Posted speed limits (for example 30 zones) apply to everyone.

3. Alcohol

  • In Swiss road law, you are drunk from 0.5 per mille blood alcohol (0.25 mg/l breath); from 0.8 per mille (0.4 mg/l) it is a qualified (more serious) offence.
  • Bikes, slow e-bikes (up to 25 km/h) and e-scooters count as non-motor vehicles: riding one while unfit to ride is punishable.
  • Fast e-bikes count as motor vehicles, so the motor-vehicle drink-driving rules apply.
  • The 0.1 per mille zero-tolerance rule for learner drivers and probationary licence holders is explained on Alcohol, cannabis and tobacco rules in Vaud. It does not apply to probationary licence holders riding a vehicle of category M (which includes fast e-bikes): there the general 0.5 per mille limit applies (OCR art. 2a).

4. Insurance, fines, parking, theft and rentals

  • Liability: with no bike vignette, damage you cause on a bike is covered by your private liability (RC) insurance. Check your policy: Household and RC insurance.
